1. R501-3 F1. Each boot read of a slot feeds every byte the port returned into a CRC-32 digest, with the byte count: the header read, the container read, or every streamed chunk of a container longer than the stage. A non-OK verdict, BLANK included, stands only when an earlier read of the same slot gave the same verdict, the same count and the same digest. Two reads whose bytes differ are a media fault, counted in `read_faults`, and the slot is read again within `NVM_READ_TRIES` (3). A clean read inside that budget is judged, and OK stands on it. If no two reads agree, the slot is UNREAD and the writer is HELD (decision 2). The digest covers exactly the bytes the verdict is judged on: the 40 header bytes for a header verdict (blank, magic, version, length), and the header plus the whole container otherwise. CRC-32 always detects a difference of one or two bits, or one confined to 32 consecutive bits; R501-3's two corruptions differ by two bits of one byte. Any other difference goes undetected with a probability of about 2^-32. A new standing check, `read_disagreement`, runs R501-3's probe on every shape, 1x1 and 8x8 included. It covers the valid slot as A and as B, at SEQ 1, 5, 0x80000000 and 0xFFFFFFFF, with the fault at header byte 0 and at body offset 0x100. The reads are XOR 8, then XOR 16, then clean, and the clean read is applied. A third varying read leaves the slot UNREAD and the writer HELD. Every case continues through a change, its commit, a clean reboot, another commit and a clean reboot, and every saved value is compared each time. The planted control `refusal_by_verdict` reduces agreement to verdict equality and must fail the check.
2. R500-3 F1. The port's time base converts `timer0` clocks to microseconds exactly, at any clock in hertz: whole seconds, then the remainder times 10^6, in 64 bits. The per-call deadline in clocks is `LS_CALL_US * CONFIG_CLOCK_FREQUENCY / 10^6`, also exact: 166,666 at 83,333,000 Hz and 200,000 at 100 MHz. The whole-MHz static assertion is removed. Both fixed 100 MHz clock stand-ins are removed. The suite writes each shape's `generated/soc.h`, with `CONFIG_CLOCK_FREQUENCY` taken from the config's `sys_clk_hz` and cross-checked against the builder's emitted LiteX argv. The host model's `timer0` counts at that clock, and the RV32 arm builds against it, so the three Arty shapes are built and graded at 83,333,000 Hz. A new LiteSPI check, `port_clock`, compares the port's deadline in clocks with the clock taken from the config, and the port's elapsed time with the model clock over several counter wraps. `time_base` crosses the counter wrap at each shape's own wrap time (51.5 s at 83.333 MHz, 42.9 s at 100 MHz). The planted control `ticks_per_us_truncated` brings back the integer ticks-per-us. It is graded at an 83.333 MHz shape and must fail.
