## The focus points, answered from the code and the netlists

   - The netlists' fetch and load fault terms admit exactly the SoC's regions of their mode at every 32-bit address:

     - the grader requires exactly one trap, of cause 5 for a load and cause 1 for a fetch;

## Findings

### F1 - MINOR - Docs - the generator's decode mechanism is misstated

- **Where:**
  - `docs/design/SPLIT_CONTROL_MEMORY.md:39-40`;
  - `docs/design/SPLIT_CONTROL_MEMORY.md:389` ("The non-power-of-two `rom` and `sram` decode of #791");
  - `sw/litex/ctrl_memory.py:139-143`, the `decode_pieces` docstring;
  - the PR body's "The fault (#791)" paragraph.
- **The claim:** "The VexiiRiscv generator checks each fetch and load against each region it is handed with one mask compare, `(address & ~(size - 1)) == origin`. That compare is exactly the region only when the size is a power of two and the origin a multiple of it."
- **Evidence:**
  - **The generator's source.** The pinned generator builds each region's PMA terms with SpinalHDL `AddressMapping.terms` (`Pma.scala`; `Misc.scala:54-80` quoted in `receipts/probes/generator_terms.txt`). That code already splits every `SizeMapping` into aligned power-of-two terms. The one exception is a region based at address 0, where `step = if(ptr == 0) m.size`: the whole size becomes one term. So only a non-power-of-two region at origin 0 gets a non-contiguous mask.
  - **The as-built netlist agrees.** `VexiiRiscvLitex_18be4f88...`, the same content as the executor's `831c88b0` apart from its name, decodes:
    - `rom` as `(A & 32'hfffc8000) == 0`;
    - `sram`, which is just as non-power-of-two (224 KiB at `0x1000_0000`), exactly, as `(A & 32'hfffe0000) == 32'h10000000`, `(A & 32'hffff0000) == 32'h10020000` and `(A & 32'hffff8000) == 32'h10030000`.
  - **The PR's own text agrees.** `tb/verilator/ctrl_region_decode/README.md:95` and `run.py:135-137` say that the single-mask netlist's `sram` terms are exact.
  - So the design doc and the source docstring each state a behaviour the generator does not have, and line 389 names `sram` as part of a defect that `sram` never had.
- **Why MINOR and not RESIDUE:**
  - This is a wrong statement of fact about the root cause, not a matter of phrasing. It sits in the authoritative design document and in a source comment, whose rule is to state behaviour and its reason.
  - It also frames the decision record for option (a) against (b): the real generator defect is the `ptr == 0` branch of the term decomposition, not a missing range compare.
  - Under the owner rule, a defect that is not clearly wording-only is MINOR.
- **Impact:**
  - A maintainer who trusts the doc expects every non-power-of-two region to be broken, and a region at 0 to be the only one that matters by accident rather than by mechanism.
  - At a re-pin they would look for the wrong behaviour, and they cannot tell from the doc that a future non-power-of-two region at origin 0, outside `add_ctrl_memory`, is the only exposed case.
  - No code, test, measurement or verdict changes.
- **Required outcome:**
  - At the three tracked places, state the pinned generator's actual behaviour: it decomposes each region into aligned power-of-two terms, except that a region based at 0 is taken whole, so the 224 KiB `rom` at 0 became `(address & 0xfffc8000) == 0` while `sram` at `0x1000_0000` was already exact.
  - Then say that handing it aligned pieces makes every region exact without depending on that decomposition.
  - Line 389 names `rom` only.
  - The PR body follows.
- **Verification:** re-read the three places at the fix head against `receipts/probes/generator_terms.txt` and the as-built netlist's terms.
