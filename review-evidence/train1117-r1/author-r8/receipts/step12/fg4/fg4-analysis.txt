# F-G4 (the split's PHY advertising every mode): the analysis at 967b1ca9, read only. No commit, no image built.

## 1. The split's write path against the release's (which shares #840's design)
- Frame: identical. Read: 32 ones, then ST 01, OP 10, PHYAD, REGAD (14 bits), released for TA and 16 data bits.
  Write: 32 ones, then ST 01, OP 01, PHYAD, REGAD, TA 10 and 16 data bits, all 64 driven.
  - Split: sw/firmware/ctrl/plat/ctrl_plat_phy.c take() and frame(); ctrl_plat_phy.h WRITE_OP 0x5, WRITE_TA 0x2.
  - Release: sw/firmware/milan_baremetal/milan_baremetal.c phy_mdio_write(): (5 << 28) | (addr << 23) | (reg << 18) | (2 << 16) | data.
- Pins: identical. The CSR is LiteEth's LiteEthPHYMDIO, with MDC in bit 0, OE in bit 1 and the driven bit in bit 2,
  as both firmwares use. The image's addresses are 0xF0001004 (w), 0xF0001008 (r) and 0xF000101C (link status), from
  the export's csr.csv (route image ctrl_plat_map_gen.h). The AX7101 pads have each port's own MDIO: e1 is J17/L16,
  e2 is AB21/AB22 (sw/litex/platforms/alinx_ax7101.py).
- Address: identical. Both probe PHYID1 from address 0 upward, one address per opportunity.
- The advertisement: identical values. Register 9 is written with its MASTER-SLAVE bits and 1000BASE-T full duplex.
  Register 4 is written without bits 5 to 9 and 14. Register 0 is written with (BMCR | AN enable | restart), reset and
  isolate cleared. Both are gated on BMSR bit 8 and ESTAT bit 13.
- Differences:
  - the bit: the split folds the release's third write (MDC low, the same data) into the next bit's first write, so
    MDC falls and the data changes in one CSR write. Data is still set a half period before each rising edge and held
    a half period after it;
  - the half period: the split uses a calibrated spin, the release cdelay(32);
  - pacing: the split takes one frame per step call (THE STEP) rather than three writes back to back;
  - re-verification: the split's advertisement is owed again after a published link loss and after THE OUTCOME.

## 2. The models: the writes land in every one
- The host test (sw/firmware/ctrl/test/phy_peer.hpp, test_plat_phy.cpp) is a pin-level Clause-22 PHY that sets a
  register only when a well-formed write reaches its address. Its arms pass at the head.
- The split boot simulation (tb/verilator/ctrl_split_boot) runs the real platform image on the real split CPU and bus.
  - Its MDIO stand-in (boot_soc.py) is the same logic as LiteEth's LiteEthPHYMDIO: storage straight to the pins, the
    input through a MultiReg. The PHY is at address 1.
  - At 967b1ca9, both shapes: 59 frames, and 7 writes landed. The boot's are (9, 0x200), (4, 0x1), (0, 0x1200), and
    after each of 2 losses (9, 0x200), (4, 0x1).
  - MDC's shortest high is 950 ns and low 1120 ns, with a period of 2260 ns (22.2.2.13: 160, 160, 400).
  - (receipts/step12/gates/splitboot/)
- So the pin sequence the split CPU puts out is a correct Clause-22 write frame, at its address, with conservative
  timing.

## 3. What the board is known to do
- CORRECTED 2026-11-19: this bullet first said that #590's hardware probe found the board's RTL8211E at address 1.
  That was wrong. #590's `mdio-phase.log` is the reviewer probe `probe_mdio_phase.py` run against a simulated peer:
  its `true=` values (PHYID1 0x001c, BMSR 0x796d) and `phy_address=1` are the model's, not the board's (#590's
  HANDOFF, "Each sample follows..." and the probe's PHASE=0/1 peer conventions). No record in the repository or
  #590 gives a board PHY address. The board's own reading of 2026-11-19 has address 0 answering, holding the
  writes, and reporting the e2 link.
- #840 (the advertisement's design, merged by #842) states "No hardware was used". No record found of the release
  image's advertisement measured on hardware after #840:
  - an 11-07 log predates the merge;
  - the bench's 11-15 rehearsal ran an image without #919's poll.
  So the release's advertisement is also unproven on this board.
- LiteEth's PHY CRG (with_hw_init_reset=True, liteeth/phy/gmii.py and common.py LiteEthPHYHWReset) holds the PHY's
  reset pin low for 256 sys cycles at FPGA start, 2.56 us at 100 MHz. The RTL8211E's datasheet minimum is in
  milliseconds.
  - The split's first frames follow within milliseconds: its boot is ready at about 28 ms at 1x1/1if.
  - The release's follow the LiteX BIOS's boot, much later.

## 4. What the bench's evidence does and does not show
- The partner's view (registers 5 and 10 at the test machine) holds every mode, after the cold boot and at later
  negotiations. A 10BASE-T-half-only partner got a 10 Mb/s link, 5 of 5. No renegotiation storm was seen. So no
  write and no restart took effect on the wire, at boot or later.
- The DUT's LINK counters follow eff_link = i_link_up & cfg_sw_link & (cfg_linkg_dis | linkg_est). The "+2 per flap"
  the bench counted is the hardware link guard's pair (hdl/milan/milan_datapath.sv:3404-3414). A link never
  established at 10 Mb/s by the GMII guard reads as no LINK_UP whatever the poll publishes.
- The bench never read MAC_STATUS (0x110) or the published link word. So it is not known whether the poll publishes,
  or is past its probe, on the board.

## 5. Candidates, and what tells them apart
- (a) The poll's writes reach the PHY and it does not apply them, while its reads work. One mechanism is boot timing:
  frames within milliseconds of LiteEth's 2.56 us reset pulse, against the release's BIOS-delayed start. That alone
  does not explain why the writes after a later link loss do not take either.
- (b) The poll is not running, or not publishing, on the board after boot. i_link_up would then sit at its reset
  value (up, 1000 Mb/s, full) and the guard alone gate the link. That fits every bench observation, but nothing in
  the models reproduces it.
- (c) The PHY's handling of a write frame differs from IEEE 802.3 22.2.4.5's model in a way that spares reads, for
  example the folded MDC-low write, or the restart's value. Code reading finds no such defect, and the RTL
  simulation lands every write.
- One boot reading the poll's state would tell them apart:
  - its address and found flag;
  - its stage, `adv` and `renegotiations`;
  - a count of polls started;
  - the last published word;
  - the last read-back of registers 4 and 9.
  The bench has no tool for it. A small console field on milan_status would carry it, with no mailbox contract change.
