### Findings for the reviewers and the manager

1. **Acceptance 3's negative control cannot fail the way it is worded.** "a negative control (re-base disabled) shows the drift back and fails": disabling the step's re-centre leaves the render fill constant (the control above breaks only the count). The #386 stage is timed from the PDU's accept, not from its presentation time, and late or early PDUs are still accepted, so a PHC step moves nothing it holds. Its failing arms are the count and a mis-snapping re-centre. This needs a public decision (amend the wording to "the count and the constant", or name a PHC-dependent media element that drifts); I did not reinterpret it privately.
2. **`mr` toggles can cancel.** `KL_media_clock_restart` flips one target per request. Under CRF selection, a CRF disruption and a step landing inside the 8-PDU hold would flip it twice and put nothing on the wire. The gmstep scenario has no CRF disruption (the CRF sink's lock depends on sequence continuity only, so a timestamp jump does not unlock it). The follow-up should decide whether a re-base should coalesce with a pending restart.
3. **CRF servo guard gap (derived from reading, not simulated).** `KL_mmcm_drp_servo` discards a 512 ms window only above 1024 ppm; the legitimate bound is about 211 ppm. A step between about 108 us and 524 us therefore lands in neither class and is integrated as a rate error. Under the new policy every locked step is above 100 us, so this band is reachable. Pre-existing (the old 20 us rule reached it too). Proposed as a new issue, not filed from this lane.
4. The talker in gmstep is opened by `AAF_CTRL[1]`, not an lwSRP licence (no SRP peer in the leg). The licence checks grade that the gate stays open and the stream continues.
5. The model's render constant is 9 events (setpoint + 1, inside the #386 band) because a PDU's ingress spans most of a 2 MHz media tick; the 100 MHz legs sit on 8. The talker's cadence in this model is its own (about 642 cycles per PDU), so it is graded against its baseline rate.
