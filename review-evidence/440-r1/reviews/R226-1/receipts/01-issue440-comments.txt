=================== 5651705425
[A10] Widening this issue's scope, from a measurement in the round-9 review of PR #428 (published there).

The issue as written treats the fence and type-1 closers as one family: positions where the walk reads Python's notion of whitespace and the renderer reads spaces and tabs. At the type-1 closers there are two more row families, and neither is a whitespace divergence at all.

**Blanks inside the closing tag.** The renderer ends a type-1 block only on a line containing the literal `</pre>`, `</script>`, `</style>` or `</textarea>`. The walk ends it on a pattern that admits a run of whitespace before the closing angle bracket, so `</pre >` and `</pre` plus a tab plus `>` end the block for the walk and do not end it for the renderer. The review measured these through the shipped gate at the base, at the round-8 head and at the round-9 head: each stays exempt, and the renderer shows no such heading.

**Case folding.** The walk matches the closing name case-insensitively under Python's Unicode rules, where the renderer folds ASCII only. Under Python's rules `s` also matches U+017F and `i` also matches U+0131 and U+0130, so a closing tag spelled with those characters ends the block for the walk alone. The review measured two such rows end to end, and they are in no issue today.

**What changes here.** The acceptance stays as written, with the measured rows extended: the fence rows and the type-1 rows this issue already names, plus the blank-inside-the-tag rows and the case-folded rows. Whichever answer is chosen, reading the renderer's class or refusing what the walk cannot answer for, it has to cover all four families, and the arms have to fail when any one of them is narrowed back.

Nothing on the tree is affected today: no tracked page carries any of these shapes, and the same was true when the issue was raised.
