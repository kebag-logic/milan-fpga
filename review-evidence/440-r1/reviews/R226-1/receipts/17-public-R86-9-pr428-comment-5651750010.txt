## Does the termination claim hold?

Partly, and not as stated. The round asked me to try to break it four ways.

**A decision site the enumeration does not see: YES, several.**
`_class_guards()` collects sites from two places only: module-level
`ast.Assign` nodes with `ast.Name` targets that contain a `.compile(` call
(`scripts/gen_toc.py:854`), and `ast.Call` nodes with an `ast.Attribute` func
inside the functions reachable from `WALK_ROOTS` (`scripts/gen_toc.py:858`).
Measured on synthetic sources (`probe_guard.out`) and then on the tracked file
(`controls.out`):

- an annotated binding, `X: re.Pattern = re.compile(...)`, is not an
  `ast.Assign`, so the site vanishes. Control C3 rebinds `BLOCK_QUOTE_RE` that
  way on the tracked file: `--sites` drops from 26 to 25, no `GUARD` note is
  printed, and `--selftest` still reports `PASS (267/267 arm(s))`. No arm pins
  the site count: `_class_guard_arms()` asserts that the note list is empty and
  that both kinds are present, never how many sites there are.
- a tuple-target assignment and `from re import compile` lose the site the same
  way (probes A2 and A3).
- a decision reached through a module-level alias or a module-level lambda is
  outside the call walk (probes A11 and A13).

**A spelling the guard does not refuse: YES.**
The guard refuses a hand-spelled class in a compiled expression and in a
`strip`, and nothing else. Measured:

- a membership test. Control C1 replaces `char not in CLASSES["blank"]` with
  `char not in " \t"` at `scripts/gen_toc.py:277`, the walk's own indentation
  decision: 26 sites, no `GUARD` note, `PASS (267/267)`. Control C2 widens the
  same inline class with a character of its own: same result. That decision is
  the one that says whether a line is an indented code block, which is what
  gates the type-7 escape under a heading, and it is neither enumerated nor
  judged.
- `str.startswith`, `endswith`, `replace`, `find`, `count`, `partition`,
  `index`, `removeprefix`, `translate` and `split` WITH an argument all carry a
  class past the guard (probes A6 and A7, control C5). A bare `split()` is a
  refusal site, so the asymmetry is deliberate for `strip` and accidental
  everywhere else.
- inside an expression the guard does judge, a blank flanked by two
  alphanumerics is exempted by the heuristic at `scripts/gen_toc.py:846`, so
  `[a b]` passes (probes A8 and A9). That exemption exists for `GENERATED_RE`'s
  prose and is reasonable; it is simply not airtight.
