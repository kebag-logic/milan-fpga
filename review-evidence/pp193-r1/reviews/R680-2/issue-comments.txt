[A10] Ruling on the [A661] STOP (two Listener declarations on the wire for one StreamID; IEEE 802.1Q-2018 10.7.7 and the 35.2.6 NOTE). The fix is one declaring applicant per StreamID, done by owner election inside the existing per-sink plane. It is not a rewrite of the plane.

1. **Owner.** For each StreamID that settled sinks declare, exactly one sink is the declaration owner: the lowest-index settled sink declaring it. Only the owner's applicant transmits. The other sinks declaring the same StreamID are riders: their applicants stay quiet (no JoinMt, no New, no Lv on their own teardown) while an owner exists.
2. **Handover.** When the owner tears down or rebinds away and riders remain, ownership moves to the next rider with the applicant state carried over: no Lv!, no New!, and the declaration continues uninterrupted (IEEE 802.1Q-2018 35.1.2.2: the stream is still wanted). The last declarer's teardown follows the #188 rules unchanged: Lv!, owed Leave, park, settle arms.
3. **Declared type.** The owner declares the combination over every sink still declaring the StreamID: Ready, AskingFailed, or ReadyFailed when both occur (IEEE 802.1Q-2018 35.2.6, 35.2.2.8.7; Milan v1.2 4.2.7). A rider's change of type updates the owner's declaration.
4. **Registrar side and ACMP:** unchanged per sink. Each sink still reports its own GET_RX_STATE and registration view.
5. **Suite cases**, each with a planted mutant that is KILLED:
   - two sinks on one stream: exactly ONE VectorAttribute per value on the wire, checked with frame bytes as in the STOP;
   - unbind the owner first, then the rider: no Lv, the declaration continues, then one Lv;
   - the reverse order;
   - three sinks;
   - a rider rebind to another stream;
   - the type combination: Ready with AskingFailed gives ReadyFailed;
   - a Milan peer registrar (4.2.7.2.2) never deregisters while a sink wants the stream.
