[A188] Step the PHC only on a first synchronization over one second (#68)

Branch `68-step-policy`, one commit `77c34e904eba2446755d13e1bf4763d1652c3ca7` (tree `10154fc3f95ccc542a6f5dcd8a58105d302fd1af`) on main `c1b617435824929a790739ea8585c3fe1a328cc0`.

## What changes

The plane used to step the PHC on **every** slave Sync/Follow_Up pair whose |offset| exceeded 20 us. It now does what the parent decision recorded in #68 (kebag-logic/milan-fpga#387, 2026-09-09) says:

- a pair steps only when **both** hold: it is a **first synchronization**, and |offset| **exceeds one second** (exactly 1 s slews);
- every other pair slews through the rate path;
- a grandmaster identity change makes the next pair first, so it steps only above one second: **an identity change alone never steps**.

A *first synchronization* is the pair that finds the published sync verdict (`pub_flags_o` bit 3) clear, i.e. the pair that raises it. The verdict is clear after reset, a 375 ms Sync receipt timeout, a grandmaster identity change, and this plane taking the grandmaster role. Losing asCapable stops Sync consumption, so the receipt timeout clears the verdict before asCapable can return. This reading and the slew bound below were posted as a material decision on #68: https://github.com/Mister-M-alt/FPGA-gPTP/issues/68#issuecomment-5790066720

Stated consequence: a synchronized servo never steps, whatever the offset.

### The microcode, and why it is shaped this way

The ROM had 19 free words, the SERVO leg sat in a 53-word gap (52 in the seeded regression images), and the packer is first-fit decreasing. So:

- The threshold reuses `S_1E9` (one second, already in scratch) and tests `(offset + 1 s) / (2 s + 1) == 0`, which is exactly `|offset| <= 1 s` in unsigned 64-bit arithmetic. The generator asserts `STEP_NS_C == 1_000_000_000` so a different threshold must load its own constant.
- The input saturation and the pre-existing integrator clamp share one helper `e_sat` (same exact-range test, sign picked branch-free). The integrator clamp is therefore re-encoded, 14 words -> 8, with an identical result (proved below).
- The step arm reuses the slew arm's negate-and-write, so the step still writes `-offset` then the bare integrator, in that order.

Result: SERVO grows 48 -> 52 words in place; every other leg keeps its base in all configurations tried (default, `--clk-hz 2000000`, both seeded images, 25 and 125 MHz, other MAC and p1).

**Why saturate the slew input.** Before this change the rate path never saw more than 20 us. At up to one second the signed 32 x 32 multiply overflows and the trim leaves the PHC's range (at the engine suite's 2 MHz, a 900 ms offset programs garbage). Saturating at +-20 us keeps the loop bit-identical inside the band and caps the trim at what the old loop could already program: about 120 ppm proportional plus the 200 ppm integrator clamp, about 320 ppm. Consequence: a 900 ms first offset slews for about 33 to 83 minutes for a master between +140 and -140 ppm of nominal, about 47 minutes against a nominal master (`scripts/servo_slew_model.py` in the lane record).

## Tests (engine suite, `make -C tb/verilator/engine`)

1,137 checks on the shipping image and on both seeded images (was 905). Each #68 pair is graded by one helper: published offset, the scripted first/synchronized precondition, exact step-pulse and addend-pulse counts (a pulse held two cycles counts twice), the addend against an exact-integer mirror of the documented rule, and for a step its value `-offset`, that it precedes its addend, and that `phc_step_o` holds afterwards.

| Required case | Where |
|---|---|
| 900 ms slews, no step pulse | phase 13: a first +900 ms pair, then twelve synchronized -900 ms pairs walking the integrator rail to rail; the widest trim equals the pre-#68 envelope |
| 1.1 s steps exactly once | phase 13b: a first +1.1 s pair steps once (bare-integrator addend); the same 1.1 s synchronized slews; -1.1 s steps |
| GM change, small offset, no step | phase 18c: the parent announces a new grandmaster identity; the next pair, 300 us off (far over the old 20 us), slews. Contrast: a second identity change then -1.5 s steps once |
| first-sync case | phase 10: the plane's first pair after asCapable, about 1 ms off, slews; phase 21: the first pair after asCapable returns, -1.2 s off, steps once |
| boundary | phase 13b: +-1 s slew, +-(1 s + 1 ns) step |

Existing checks that encoded the retired rule were rewritten, not deleted. The base harness on the new ROM fails exactly 15 checks:

- "re-bases the phc" x4 (phases 10, 11c, 11d x2);
- "pairs and steers" x3 (phase 11e);
- "late FU never steers" (it counted phase 10's step);
- "step is the negation" and "step writes the bare estimate" (phase 13, a 1 ms step);
- phase 14's mirror;
- phase 15's four closed-loop checks (it started 1 ms off and expected a step).

Phase 15 now starts 1.5 s off after a receipt timeout, so it still proves one re-base carrying the surviving integrator, and every pair of the loop is now graded against the mirror.

### Below the threshold the servo is unchanged: ROM-level proof

`scripts/rom_servo_differential.py` executes the SERVO leg words of the real base and new images, decoded as the microCPU defines them. Registers start as random garbage, and any unmodelled state read aborts. At 2, 25, 100 and 125 MHz: for every |offset| <= 20 us (about 6,600 random cases per clock, first and synchronized, integrator anywhere in [-ILIM, ILIM] including both rails), the two images write the identical sequence (PHC, integrator, timers, flags, COMMIT). The new image matches the #68 formula over +-2^40 ns plus edge offsets, and the base image matches the pre-#68 formula, which validates the emulator (20,080 cases each per clock).

## Observation for follow-up (pre-existing, not changed here)

The neighbour rate ratio divides by a PHC-time window, so the first Pdelay window spanning a step is distorted. Against a real peer, a forward step of about 1 to 3.3 s (window still under 2^32 ns) could push the next computed link delay past the Milan floor or threshold and drop asCapable once. This is analysis, not a measurement: this harness's peer clock is derived from our own PHC, so it cannot show it. The old policy stepped more often and in this same range, so #68 does not introduce it. It is relevant to #387's acceptance 3 (a GM change stepping more than one second).
